Records

Petersburg, 1996

Autumn ’96 came to Petersburg the way it always does, without asking: fine rain and wind off the Gulf.

The city has been Petersburg again for five years, but on the trams people still say “Leningrad” out of habit, and on peeling facades leaflets from the last election are pasted over old slogans. The markets pay for protection. Cops go half a year without pay, fuel their own UAZ patrol cars and quit one after another: some into private security, some into the gangs. Sometimes it’s the same road.

At night the only lights are the kiosks by the metro. In their windows, all jumbled together: water, Rasputin vodka, Snickers, Turbo gum with the little inserts and cigarettes sold one at a time.

Every character here shares one city: the same streets, familiar faces, shared stories, only each of them remembers it their own way. Some have old debts, some have secrets, and nobody is in a hurry to tell.

A dead man was found by the garages on Zaitseva Street. The case runs through every start and keeps growing details. You can try to work out what happened. Or start from a blank page: it’s a big city.

How to set up

Both cards are built the same way: description in English, speech and nicknames in Russian, lorebook built in.

  1. Download the card

    A PNG with the portrait or a JSON — same thing inside.

  2. Import into SillyTavern

    Character list, the import-from-file button, pick the downloaded file.

  3. Import everything it offers

    On import, the tavern will ask whether to bring in everything built into the card along with it: the lorebook, the tags and the rest. Be sure to say yes to all of it — then the lorebook links itself to the card.

  4. Pick a start

    The starts sit as swipes on the first message — flip through them with the right arrow. The last one, “Your start,” is empty: you write the opening yourself.

Then why a separate lorebook file?
In case the embedded one doesn’t load. Then: World Info, import, pick the file and link it to the character.

Sloppycame up with Vitya

Sloppy. Meant to look at just one photo and ended up going through the whole album. The tea went cold.

Oct. 96

Hi!

I’m Sloppy, and all of this is a pretty personal story for me.

I was born and raised in St Petersburg, and so were my parents. Growing up, I listened to their stories about the nineties: how they lived, how they got by, what they laugh about now. And we have plenty of photo albums from those years at home. I still love leafing through them and turning the pictures over to read the notes on the back.

I suppose that’s why I wanted to capture the atmosphere of those years – the way it lives on in family stories, photographs and old newsreels. And while I was writing Vitya, I was really inspired by the film “Brother” and the music of that time, especially the band Nautilus Pompilius.

When I was creating Vitya, it mattered to me that you could feel a whole life behind him: a childhood, a family, friends, his own mistakes and memories. I wanted him to be not a cardboard cutout, but a person you want to keep getting to know.

I wrote Vitya, and Lyosha was written by my close friend Milky. We worked on them together for a long time: talking over the little things, changing, rewriting and trying really hard. I hope you’ll feel that and appreciate it.

Into this I’ve put a piece of my heart and a piece of my city: a few family memories, a bit of my St Petersburg and a whole lot of love for these guys. I hope that as you get to know Vitya and Lyosha, you’ll also feel something of the St Petersburg that comes alive for me whenever I leaf through the old albums.

Yours, Sloppy

Milkycame up with Lyosha

Milky. The caption was supposed to be longer, but Lyosha borrowed the pen again and never gave it back.

Oct. 96

Hello-hello!

Huge thanks to everyone who waited for Lyosha, and to everyone who dared to play with him!

The idea came after watching “Maloy” – a fairly recent Russian series on exactly this theme. Not much from our film industry appeals to me (God save us from that kolobok), but I do recommend this one – it’s really funny, plenty to giggle at.

At first I wanted to make a young cop, but then Sloppy showed me an old copypasta from Pikabu about typical NTV cop shows. The main character there was Alexei Tikhonov – a typical cop. And, as you can see, here’s what came of it, haha.

And even though those were far from cheerful times, I really enjoyed working with this setting!

Yours, Milky
